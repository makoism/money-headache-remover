## 0. Rules for every record

- **Ids** are strings `<device part>-<session part>-<counter>`, for example `k7qzpa-m2xq7ab9zt-41`. The device part (6 letters and digits) is made once per device and kept in device storage only (never in the shared copy or a backup; a restored copy gets the new device's part for new records). The session part is 10 random characters (letters and digits), drawn on every page open, so two tabs never clash and one device does not reuse a session part in practice [O3] (3 letters gave 17,576 parts, shared by 34 to 95 pairs of opens a year on a phone opened 3 to 5 times a day, final_review_1 finding 3). The counter starts at 1 per session. Ids are never renumbered.
- **Ids built from what a record is** [O2]. A record the app writes for a known moment is named by that moment, so two devices writing it make one record and the merge keeps the newest `at`: a settlement the check-in writes (a ticked pay, bill, card payment) `set:<occurrence>` (`set:pay@2026-12-25`); a spend a schedule logs by itself `auto:<schedule>@<due>` (`auto:gym@2027-02-01`); a period-end answer `pe:<the occurrence of the pay that started the period>:<place>` (`pe:pay@2026-11-25:free`), one record per place holding all its moves [P2, T1]; a period started without the pay `ps:<the occurrence it stands in for>` (`ps:pay@2026-12-25`), with the day the person chose as its `date`, so two phones make one record and the newer `at` wins [N5, T1]; a period is never named by a day that can move [T1]; the move to Waiting written when a place with money is removed `rm:<place>` (`rm:trip`) [Q7]; a closing's transfer out or write-off `close:<account>` (`close:old`) [Q7], and when the closing writes several records `close:<account>:1`, `:2`, in the order written (`close:old:1`, `close:old:2`) [S3]; the skip written when a self-logged spend is removed `skip:<occurrence>` (`skip:gym@2027-02-08`) [Q5]; a fee record `fee:<the id of its transfer>` (`fee:set:top@2027-01-05`, `fee:k7qzpa-m2xq7ab9zt-41`), so two phones ticking one transfer with a fee write one fee record [X2]; every record the move writes `mv:<kind>:<the 38.31 id or key it came from>` (`mv:rc:r1700000000123`, `mv:card:c1`, `mv:debt:d1`), and a record with no 38.31 id `mv:<kind>:<the field path it came from>` (`mv:acct:cfg`, `mv:open:cfg`, `mv:check:cfg.bankStart`, `mv:pay:steps.a`, `mv:person:ui.name`) [Y28], so the move run on the phone and on the desktop from the same 38.31 copy makes one copy of each record after the merge [X1]; the kinds are those of MOVE_FIELDS.md section 7 [Z] (a 38.31 month-end answer is a period-end answer and takes its `pe:` id, section 11.1). A built id may name a moved record: `set:mv:pay:steps.a@2026-12-25`, `pe:mv:pay:steps.a@2026-11-25:mv:place:trip`, `rm:mv:place:trip`, `close:mv:acct:cfg` (rules mg-87 to mg-95). So a goal removed, an account closed or a self-logged spend removed on two devices before they sync is one record, never two (final_review_2 S11). Everything a person types gets an ordinary id. A built id taken back with Undo is the same record marked `gone`; writing the same moment again is an edit of it.
- **Moments** [T watch]. `told` and `at` are compared as moments, never as text: each carries its offset, so a record told at 09:30 in Manila (10:30 in Tokyo) comes after one told at 10:00 in Tokyo. **Start and end of a day** [X9]: "the start of its day" (a moved receipt with no time, a record dated with no time) and "the end of its day" (an opening that covers its whole day) are in the offset of the device that wrote the record: for a moved record, the moving device's (the copy's last save carries it). A time written with no offset of its own is in that device's offset too.
- **Dates** are `YYYY-MM-DD` in the person's local calendar. All engine date maths is on whole day numbers [D8]. "Today" is worked out again whenever the app comes back into view. Times appear only in `at`.
- **Amounts** are whole minor units (integers): yen 1 is 1, peso 1.00 is 100. The currency table is the full ISO 4217 minor-unit table, shipped with the page (every active code with its decimals: 0 for JPY, KRW, VND, ISK and the others, 2 for most, 3 for BHD, IQD, JOD, KWD, LYD, OMR, TND, 4 for CLF and UYW; `rules/units_ref.js` holds it) [L units q1]; an unknown code is 2 decimals and the screen asks once. A typed amount with more decimals than its currency has is rounded half up and the screen says so [L units q2]. Nothing is rounded on load: a saved amount that is not whole is kept as it is and flagged [L units q9].
- **Currency** is an ISO code on every account, schedule, goal and budget line, and on every stored amount that is not on an account: `plan.shares` entries, `home.minBank` and `plan.limits` carry `cur` [O6]. The home currency is dated: `home.curs: [ { from, cur, at, by } ]`, the entry in force on a day being the latest dated on or before it [O6]. A change of home currency (someone working in Japan going home to the Philippines) starts a new entry; nothing is converted and history stays in its own currency, so old entries stay readable [O6, which replaces E2's "convert every amount"]. An account's own currency never changes once it has events. "$" and "¥" are asked, never guessed.
- **`at`** on every record is a hybrid clock: the larger of now and (the newest `at` this device has seen plus 1 ms), written as ISO with the time zone offset [D4]. A now more than a day ahead of the newest `at` this device has seen is held at that newest `at` plus 1 ms, and a warning says the device's clock is ahead [L merge q8]. The same holds a copy that arrives: every `at` in it more than a day ahead of the newest `at` the receiving device has seen is held at that point plus 1 ms, and the warning is shown [M4] (section 2.3). A held `at` is marked on the record as `held: { came, at }`, the `at` it came with and the `at` it was held at (a `steps` or `rules` entry carries its own mark; a shared `ui` key's mark is in `ui.held`), and an edit keeps the mark like any field. A record held once keeps its held `at`: a copy of it whose `at` is the mark's `came` is that same far-ahead write, so it counts at the held `at` and is never held a second time, and the fast device takes the held value when the merged copy comes back, so a later edit on the other device is never lost to it [N1]. A wrong clock on one device cannot always win. A record's `at` moves only when the record changes (an edit, a removal, a Redo); a merge keeps the winning copy's `at` [L merge q3]. **A moved record** (one the move writes from an old copy) carries the copy's last save as its `at` (section 11, AA10), never the move moment, so a real edit made after the move on any device wins over the same record moved later on another device [AB1].
- **`by`** on every record, with `at`: the device part of its last writer (section 0, Ids). On an equal `at`, after `gone` [H6], the larger `by` wins [L merge q1]. The id's device part still names the device that made the record. Each `steps` and `rules` entry carries its own `by` beside its own `at`, and each shared `ui` key has its `by` in `ui.by` beside `ui.at` [M5].
- **`told`** on every record [H1]: when the app first heard of it, as the same hybrid clock. It is set once, when the record is made, and never changed by an edit, an Undo, a Redo or a merge (a merge keeps the oldest `told` it sees for an id). A copy that arrives with a `told` more than a day ahead of the newest `at` the receiving device has seen has it held the same way as `at`, at that point plus 1 ms [N2]; as the oldest `told` is kept, a record keeps its first held `told` on both devices. It orders records that share a date: a check holds the events dated before its day and those on its day whose `told` is before the check's `told` (section 7.1). An import that replaces a typed line keeps that line's `told` [O1]; an unmatched import line, or a spend that logs itself written late, dated on a check's day or the setup day counts as inside that figure whatever its `told`, and a printed running balance (`src.bal`) decides the order instead when present (section 7.1) [O1].
- **Nothing is deleted.** Remove and Undo set `gone: '<date>'` and `goneBy: <id of the step or record that removed it>`, and move `at`. Redo clears both and moves `at` again, so the Redo is newer than the Undo [H6]. Undo is these marks; there is no full copy inside the saved data [D2].
- **Nothing is dropped for being out of range.** No fixed window; a date years back or ahead is kept as typed.
- **Unknown fields are kept** on load and save, arrays and objects too, so an older page never strips a newer field.
- **Every list record** (`plan.shares` entries, budget lines, schedules, goals, accounts, events, people, check-ins, and on a moved copy `pendingMove` entries and `frozen` texts [AB rows, AB1]) has `id`, `told`, `at` and `by`. Single records (`home`, `plan.limits`) have `at` and `by`. Each entry inside a `steps` or `rules` list carries its own `at` and `by` [L merge q2, M5], and is removed by its own `gone` with its own `at`, never by taking it out of the list [M3]; each shared `ui` key has its `at` in `ui.at` and its `by` in `ui.by` [L merge q6, M5]. Any of them that was held on arrival also carries its `held` mark (for a `ui` key, in `ui.held`) [N1].

## 1. Where it is stored [D1, D3, D5, D6, D7]

| Store | What | Written |
|---|---|---|
| IndexedDB `burrow3`, store `events` | one record per event, keyed by `id`, indexed by `date` and `acct` | per change |
| IndexedDB `burrow3`, store `book` | one record: everything in section 2 except `events` | per change |
| IndexedDB `burrow3`, store `kept` | a whole copy kept before a big replace (backup restore, cloud load), its own record, never inside `book` | on that replace only |
| IndexedDB `burrow3`, store `device` | device-only state (section 2.2) | per change |
| localStorage | small device things only: the device id part, the last open, the look | as today |

- The old 38.31 key is read once by the move and never written again, except one marker `{ movedTo: 'burrow3', at }` [D6]. Old pages that find the marker show "Update the app" and write nothing.
- **Every writer checks the version first** (local save, another tab, backup restore, cloud): if the stored `v` is newer than the page knows, the page becomes read-only with "Update the app" and keeps both copies [D6]. Tabs signal each other with BroadcastChannel.
- After setup the page calls `navigator.storage.persist()`; Settings shows whether it was granted and the space used [D7].
- The cloud copy and backup files are `{ fmt: 'gz1', data }`: the copy as JSON, gzipped, then encrypted [D5]. The cloud copy of this shape lives under its own key on the vault server, where a 38.31 page cannot load or overwrite it [E3]. Pushes happen on leaving the app, on going to the background, and at most every 1 to 2 minutes. Backups are read from the file directly, never through a text box. The move link is retired.
- The service worker precaches with `{ cache: 'reload' }` and falls back to the cache after a 3 second network timeout [D6].

## 2. The copy

### 2.1 Shared (merged across devices)
```
{ v: 3,                       // shape version; a page that knows a lower v never writes over it
  fmt: 'gz1',                 // only on the cloud copy and backup files
  rev, at,                    // revision (max(now, rev + 1)) and when saved; after a merge, the larger of both revs plus one [L merge q3]
  home: { curs: [ { from: '2026-12-11', cur: 'JPY', at, by } ], country: 'JP', mode: 'track',
          minBank: null | { a, cur }, mainBank: <account id>, at },   // [O6] the home currency dated; [O4] mainBank
  people: [ { id, name, at } ],
  accounts: [...],            // section 3
  places: [...],              // section 4 (goals and the other kept places)
  schedules: [...],           // section 5
  events: [...],              // section 6 (stored one per record, section 1)
  checkins: [...],            // section 7
  plan: { shares: [...], budgets: [...], limits: {...}, at },   // section 8
  imports: { cats: {...}, at },  // what the person taught the statement reader (38.31 ui.cats)
  ui: { ..., at: { <key>: <at> }, by: { <key>: <by> }, held: { <key>: { came, at } } },   // shared screen choices only (section 11, "shared ui"); `ui.at` and `ui.by` hold each key's `at` and `by` [L merge q6, M5]; `ui.held` a held key's mark [N1]
  pendingMove: [ { id: 'mv:pend:<field path>', field, waits, to, ym, a, ..., gone, told, at, by } ],   // [AA5, AB rows] a moved copy only: what waits (section 11)
  frozen: [ { id: 'mv:frozen:<by>', raw: '<the 38.31 copy exactly as stored>', told, at, by } ]   // [AB1] one per device that moved, read only
}
```
- `home.mode`: `'track'` (Free to spend is a place) or `'wallet'` (Free to spend is handed out of tracked accounts), as in the ledger. Track by default.
- `home.curs` [O6]: the home currency, dated. Each entry holds from its `from` to the next entry's; the first is from setup. A day before the first entry uses the first. Entries merge by `from` like `rules` entries (each with its own `at` and `by`).
- `home.minBank`: the minimum for the main bank, `{ a, cur }` [O6]. Null means no minimum. Setup asks once, with no amount filled in (MAKO M1, recommended).
- `home.mainBank`: the account that pays cards and bills by default, takes `home.minBank` [B8, A8], and holds every budget line and the Free to spend share that names no account [O4].
- `home.country` picks the holiday table and the bank-closed table [F17] for every account that has no `country` of its own (section 3).
- `frozen` [AB1]: each device that ran the move keeps the raw stored text of its own 38.31 copy (`frozen3831`, never a cleaned copy: both builds' cleaners drop data, receipts beyond 5,000 in model.js, and 38.28's writes ₱ into a copy with no currency [O]) as the record `mv:frozen:<by>`, `by` the device part, with the copy's last save as `at`. Two devices with different 38.31 copies keep both, on both devices and in the cloud; before AB1 it was one shared field with no `at`, and the merge kept each device's own (`rules/move.json` mv-86).
- `pendingMove` [AA5, AB rows]: on a moved copy, every one-month amount and month-end answer whose pay, bill or payment day is not answered yet, a month-end answer for a period not yet ended at the move (that period's own question still comes, with it offered as the default [AD3]) [AB2], a receipt on the loan line while "which loan is this" is not answered [AC2], and the loan line's one-month amount while that question waits [AC7]. Each entry is a record with the built id `mv:pend:<field path>` [Y28], `told`, `at` and `by`, merging like any record. It names the 38.31 field it came from (`field`, `once.2027-01.a`), what it waits for (`waits`: a schedule or account, `mv:pay:steps.a`, or `loanLine`), the month (`ym`), the amount in minor units (`a`) and what it becomes (`to`): `amounts` (pay: the month's total spread over that month's pays [AB2]; a bill's, a debt's or the loan line's: that month's first occurrence, the whole payment [Y7, AC5, AC7]), `once` (with `line`: on that budget line, spread over the periods starting in the month [AB2]), `shares` (with `share` or `goal`: `once` on that period's `plan.shares` entries, spread the same way), `move` (with `place`: the Emergency fund draw, id `mv:draw:once.<ym>` [AB rows, AC4]), `periodEnd` (with `place`, `moves` and `told`: the `pe:` record of the period holding the month's last day, once that period has ended) or `repay` (with `event`: the waiting receipt). Everything an entry writes has a built id or lands on a keyed field, so a second device applying the same entry writes the same record. When what it waits for is answered, the entry is written and kept, marked `gone` with the answer's `at`, so a device still holding it takes the removal at the merge; until then it counts nowhere (section 11; `rules/move.json` mv-71, mv-72, mv-91 to mv-96). **A device that applies an answer at its own move** also writes the matching entry, marked `gone` on the move day (its `at` the copy's last save, as every moved record), so a device that moved the same copy without that answer drops its waiting copy of it at the merge (same `at`: `gone` wins, H6) and does not add it from the cloud [AD5] (mv-98, mv-99). **A waiting entry is applied only when the record it would change has an older `at` than the entry** (or is not there yet); otherwise it is skipped, and either way it is then kept marked `gone` [AD5] (mv-106 to mv-108). A waiting month-end answer is offered as the default at its period's own question, capped at what the place holds; the person confirms or changes it [AD3] (section 7.4).

### 2.3 Merging two copies [D4]
Union of every list by `id`; per record the newest `at` wins. A copy with `gone` wins only against a copy of the same record whose `at` is the same or older; a Redo (a newer `at` without `gone`) wins over the Undo it reverses [H6]. On an equal `at` with both alive, the larger `by` wins [L merge q1]. **Restoring an older backup while the cloud copy is on** [AB watch]: a union merge would bring every newer record back at the next sync, so the restore writes `gone` (the restore's day) with a new `at` for every record newer than the backup (not in it), and gives the backup's records a new `at`; inside them the backup's `steps` and `rules` entries get the new `at` too and an entry not in the backup is removed by its own `gone` [M3]; `told` stays the first one each record had. The shared `ui` keys take the backup's values with a new `at` and `by` too (`ui.at`, `ui.by`) [AD8]. The merge then keeps the restore on either side (`rules/merge.json` mg-96 to mg-99). A shared `ui` key set after the backup (not in it) has no `gone` of its own and comes back at the merge (open, mg-q13). Inside the winning record, `steps` and `rules` entries merge by `from`, each by its own `at`, so two devices adding different steps keep both [L merge q2]; an entry is settled the way a record is: the newer `at`, then on the same `at` the one with `gone`, then the larger `by` [M3, M5]. A removed entry keeps its `gone` in the list, so the other copy cannot bring it back [M3]. Shared `ui` merges key by key, by `ui.at`, then on the same `at` the larger `by` in `ui.by` [L merge q6, M5]. Before merging a copy that arrives, the receiving device holds every `at` in it more than a day ahead of the newest `at` it has seen, at that point plus 1 ms, marks it `held: { came, at }`, and shows the warning [M4, N1]; `told` is held the same way [N2]; a device that has seen nothing holds nothing. A record held once keeps its held `at` [N1]: when two copies of the same thing meet and one carries a mark whose `came` is the other's `at`, the other counts at the held `at` and takes the mark. So the same far-ahead copy arriving again is not held a second time (and shows no new warning), and the fast device takes the held value when the merged copy comes back, so a later edit on the other device wins on both. An edit newer than a removal brings the record back, and the next check-in shows it as "came back" [L merge q4]. `told` is never taken from the winning copy: the earliest `told` seen for the id is kept, and a copy with none takes the other's [H1, L merge q7]. The merged copy's `rev` is the larger of both plus one; a record's `at` moves only when it changes [L merge q3]. On a cloud conflict the page downloads, merges, and sends again with the server's version as its base; it never asks "keep mine". Period-end answers of two places that each device allowed can add up to more than is left once merged: the newer one gives way (section 7.4) [P3]; so does every share-out from a limited amount, placing money to place and covering an overspend found later [X5] (`rules/period_end.json` share_out_merged). The same amount typed on two devices on the same day for the same place is offered to the person as a possible double [H9]; the merge itself never removes either (section 6.4). The merge has its own test file (section 12).

## 3. Accounts
```
{ id, name, type, cur, owner, open: <event id>, minBank, spendFrom, tracked, closed, preset, confirm,
  postingDays,               // working days the account needs to post a line; default 1, a card 7 [F4, L matching q4]
  country,                   // its own holiday and bank-closed tables; left out: home.country [O]
  gone, at }
```
- `type`: `bank`, `cash`, `ewallet`, `wallet`, `investment` [B10], `card`, `paylater`, `loan`, `owedToYou`.
- `owner`: a `people` id, or null for shared.
- `minBank`: this account's minimum, `{ a, cur }` in the account's currency. Null: the main bank takes `home.minBank`, any other account has none.
- `country` [O]: the account's holiday and bank-closed tables: a Japanese bank and a Philippine e-wallet each move their own items, card payment days and posting windows by their own country. Left out: `home.country`.
- `spendFrom`: may safe to spend count this account's headroom. Default true for bank, cash, ewallet, wallet; false for investment, owedToYou and an account that backs a goal (`keptIn.mode 'ownAccount'`) [confirmed in W; in the reference model].
- `tracked`: false only for the Wallet-mode wallet (ledger).
- `closed` (date), `preset` (a presets file id, names only), `confirm` (set by the move: ask at the first check-in), as in the ledger.
- **Close, never delete** [O]. An account with history is closed, not removed: closing writes a final `check` (its final figure) and sets `closed`; its events stay, so the bank's history and the places it counted in never change. Only an account with no events (an Undo of adding it) is ever marked `gone`.
- **Closing** [P6, R3, R4, Q watch item]. Before anything else, closing the account a card is paid from (`payFrom`), or a place is kept in (`keptIn.acct`), asks for the new account for each and moves the link (the card's `payFrom`, the place's `keptIn.acct`); it cannot close until one is picked for each [R4]. A card, pay-later or loan that still owes money, or a bank below zero, cannot be closed until it is at zero: the closing screen offers the payment that gets it there (a `repay` from its `payFrom`, or for a bank a transfer in) [R3]. An account is closed only when its final figure is zero, or is moved out by a `transfer` the closing asks for (to another account, dated the closing day, written with the final check) [P6], or, when a balance cannot be moved out (a few pesos in a dead e-wallet), written off as a `spend` from it dated the closing day [Q watch item]. The closing's transfer or write-off has the built id `close:<account>` [Q7]. A closing that writes several records (most moved out and the rest written off, or money moved to two accounts) names them `close:<account>:1`, `:2`, in the order written: the transfers in the order the closing lists them, then the write-off [S3]. Otherwise the closing is refused and the screen shows what is still in it; nothing is written. **Closing the main bank** [T watch]: it first asks for the new main bank and moves every link to it: `home.mainBank`, the `acct` of the budget lines and of Free to spend's share held on it (or naming none), and every schedule tied to it, which moves instead of ending: pay landing in it, bills paid from it [V], transfers out of it and into it, and self-logged spends paid from it [W1] (the side on the old main bank moves); at the closing the person can end any of them, and the ones they end get `ends` set to the closing day. A transfer schedule between the old and the new main bank ends at the closing, as it would be a transfer from the new main bank to itself [Z4] (rules acc-22). The opening split stays on the account it was typed on. **Money into a closed account** after its closing day (a refund, money sent to it) reopens it, with a notice; its ended schedules stay ended. Its schedules end on the closing day (any account but the main bank, whose schedules move, above): each schedule paid from it, landing in it, or a transfer into it gets `ends` set to that day, unless it already ends on or before it. From then on the account holds nothing, has no minimum and is not counted in safe to spend. 38.31 has no closed accounts (one bank figure), so nothing closed is brought in at the move [R5]. Rules file: `rules/accounts.json`.
- **investment** [B10]: holds checks like a bank; its value may move on its own between checks (the gap is a market move, not a filler, and never raises the large-gain question [T watch]). Not counted in safe to spend (`spendFrom: false`).

**Owed accounts (card, paylater, loan) own their payment day** [B5, E6, 10.8]. There is no separate schedule for a card or loan payment.
- **card**: `inFull` (true unless it carries a balance; a card that carries one pays its `minRule` plus its `extraSteps` each payment day, and the rest is carried to the next statement [H7]), `cutDay` (1 to 31, `'last'`, or null for "not sure": then on each payment day everything owed, less payments since, is due [H4]), `payDay` (1 to 31), `payMonths` (months after the close the payment falls; 1 by default, 0 for a card paid the same month), `shift` (`'before'|'after'|'none'`; from the country by default), `payFrom` (the account that pays it; `home.mainBank` by default), `apr`, `minRule` (`{ pct, floor }` or `{ fixed }`), `of` (for an older balance: the card's id; an instalment plan is its own pay-later account, below), `extraSteps: [ { from, amount } ]` (planned extra payments; 38.31 debt `extra`) [B5], `cashApr` (the yearly rate on cash taken on the card; stored now, used in stage 2) [O5].
- **Money out of a card** [O5]: a `transfer` or `borrow` out of a card (a cash advance into the bank, cash taken at an ATM, a closed card's credit sent back to the bank) is a card line: what is owed moves by it and the next payment follows. A credit sent back brings what is owed back to zero.
- **A card statement** [H3] is what was carried over from the last statement, less the payments and refunds since its close, plus the lines posted in the cycle. Paying a statement in part leaves the rest in the next one. A statement at or below zero is a credit carried to the next one: nothing is due and no expected item is made. **Carried over** is only what is left of statements not paid by their own payment day [I3]: a statement still waiting for its payment day (a card paid two months after its close, common in Japan) is never folded into the next one, so two statements can wait at once, each with its own item. When two statements are unpaid at once, each item asks only for what the older one does not already hold, so nothing is counted twice. **Payments** [I3, confirmed in J]: a payment goes to the statement it settles (or the one that statement was carried into), then to the oldest statement still owing, then to the open cycle; so the on-time payment of a statement that was waiting does not lower the next statement to close, and a late payment of a statement already carried lowers the statement it was carried into. A statement item asks what is left of the statement after payments that settled no item, not the printed figure. **Interest** [I2]: at each close, on what is carried over after payments (a credit carries none), `apr` / 12, rounded half up to the minor unit; it is part of that statement. The look ahead adds the interest the next close will charge on what stays carried after the planned payments to the card's next open payment [confirmed in J]. **A known close day and no typed last statement** [X6]: the first close after the opening holds the opening balance plus what came after, and is paid on its payment day; the statements follow from there. **No fixed horizon** [X7]: close days, payment days, occurrences and statements are made as far as any question asks, never only to a fixed number of months; a test asks ten years out (far_years.json). **Close day not sure** [H4]: each payment day is an expected item for everything owed then (less payments since), with or without a typed last statement; after each payment the next payment day gets its own item. Such a card that carries a balance gets its interest on each payment day [J1], `apr` / 12, rounded half up, added before that day's payment, and on a payment day gone by even when its payment is not seen (as on a loan). It is on everything owed before that day's payment, spending since the last payment day included [K1]. With a percent `minRule` the day's interest is added first and the minimum is worked out on the balance including it, in the look ahead too [K2].
- **A card paid from an account in another currency** [H5]: each payment carries `a` (what left the paying account, in its currency) and `a2` (what reached the card). Until stage 2 the walk does not take the card's payments off the paying account (it cannot convert, F11) and Home shows a warning under the headline; a recorded payment takes its typed `a` off the paying account. **In the lists** [W2]: its payments are listed apart in the card's own currency and left out of the totals of "due before next pay", "not seen yet" and the check-in list, as pay in another currency is (S6). **A loan or pay-later account paid from an account in another currency** is handled the same way [V2]: its payments are left out of the paying account's walk, with a warning (`loan_other_currency`), until stage 2, and listed apart in their own currency, out of the totals [W2]; a recorded payment takes its `a` off the paying account and its `a2` off what is owed.
- **paylater** [F19, Q3]: `payDay`, `shift`, `payFrom`, `plans: [ { id, amount, count, first, at, by } ]` (a list, so one account holds several plans at once, as GGives, Atome or Paidy do: each plan is `count` parts of `amount`, monthly from `first`; the buy is a `spend` on this account and raises what it owes; each payment day asks the parts of every plan due since the payment day before, never more than is owed; each part paid is a `repay` from `payFrom`), `apr`, `ends`, `of`. What is still to come is shown as owed.
- **A card instalment buy** [Q3] (a laptop of 120,000 in 12 parts on the card) is saved on its own `paylater` account with `of: <card id>` and a plan in its `plans`. It has no payment day of its own: each part is posted to the card on its day (`first`, then monthly) as a card line, lowering what the plan owes and raising what the card owes, so each statement asks only the part posted in its cycle, never the whole buy (final_review_2 S9). **Parts not yet posted** [S4] are walked on their posting dates: each comes off the card's paying account in the look ahead on the day it posts (never more than the plan still owes), so they reach "Later on" [B11]; the card's own payment items hold only the parts already posted, so no part is taken twice.
- **loan**: `payDay`, `shift`, `payFrom`, `amount` (the usual payment), `steps: [ { from, amount } ]` (the payment changing from a date: a moved 38.31 "Loan or debt payment" line's steps [Y4]), `amounts: { <due date>: amount }` (on a loan or card: a moved 38.31 one-month debt amount, or the loan line's, on that month's payment occurrence [AA6, AC7]; it is that payment, whole: in place of the usual amount or minimum and any planned extra [AC5], loan_one_month_amount.json; formerly open: MOVE_FIELDS.md section 9, mv-q46), `paidBefore` (a note of what a moved pay-off goal had paid before the move; never counted [Z6]), `apr` (yearly rate in percent; on a loan or pay-later, interest is added on each payment day on what was owed before that day's payment, x `apr` / 12, rounded half up [I2]; it is added first and the payment asked after it, and it is added on a payment day gone by even when the payment is not seen [confirmed in J]), `minRule`, `ends`, `extraSteps`, `of`. Each payment day asks the usual `amount`, or what `minRule` asks on what is owed, plus the `extraSteps` amount in force on that day, never more than what is owed [H7]. A 38.31 "Loan or debt payment" bill line is matched to the loan at the move (or dropped with the person's OK), never both [E6].
- **What is owed on a loan or pay-later** [Q3]: a `borrow` from it (a credit line drawn into the bank, `acct` the loan, `acct2` the bank) or a `spend` on it raises what it owes; a `repay` into it lowers it. A payment day gone by asks on what was owed then, so a credit line set up at zero asks nothing until it is drawn on, then its usual payment (final_review_2 S3).
- **Paid by someone else** [Q watch item]: a `repay` may have no paying account (a parent pays the card straight to the card company): it lowers what the card or loan owes and takes nothing from any account of the person's (final_review_2 S10). A bill paid by someone else is a `skip`.
- A day 29 to 31 falls on the month's last day in a shorter month, for every day field [10.8].

**Opening** [B6, B7, H1]. Every account opens with one `open` event and a `check` the same day, written together in the first check-in; their `told` is the moment setup saved them. A bank opens with one figure, split by place: what is kept for goals and the other kept places in it, what is left of this period's budget lines and Free to spend [B2], and the rest in Waiting. Goal money is a place inside the bank, never a second open [B6]. A card opens with a check of what is on it now, and when known a check with `stmt: true` for its last statement (its close date and whether it was paid). A card may hold several unpaid statements at the opening (a card paid two months after its close, set up between a close and the older statement's payment day) [J5]: each gets its own `stmt: true` check with its amount, close date (`stmtDate`) and payment day (`stmtPay`), and each is its own expected item on that payment day (with the card's weekend rule). None of them is folded into another; the next close after the opening holds only what was posted since the latest of them [I3]. **Set up between a statement's close and its payment day** [AB3]: for a card, a pay-later or a loan, setup asks "Is the last statement paid?" and saves the answer as that statement's check [J5]: yes, paid (no amount asked: everything owed now is after that close); no, unpaid with the amount the person types, the box opening with everything owed filled in, so left as it is everything owed is that statement [AD6]; "don't know", unpaid holding everything owed, so it is all due on the next payment day (the safe side, as H4), and what is left of it after that payment is carried into the next statement (card_between_close_pay.json, loan_between_stmt_pay.json; `rules/edits.json` ed-13 to ed-16, ed-19). For a loan or pay-later, yes means that payment day's payment was made before setup, and "don't know" means that month's usual payment is due on the next payment day, not the whole balance [AD7] (loan_stmt_dont_know.json; ed-20, ed-21). Set up outside that gap, nothing is asked and X6 holds. Whether "no" on a loan or pay-later asks an amount, and filled with what, is open (ed-q3).

**Not gone out yet** [F20, X3]. The opening saves `notYet: [occurrence names]` (on the opening `check`): the pay and bills due from the last pay to the setup day, the setup day itself included, that had not gone out or come in at setup. Setup asks about each, a pay due that day first, then the rest by due date (`rules/edits.json`). Each item in `notYet` is not inside the opening, though it landed on or before it: it stays expected (late, or not seen yet, by its landing day, as any item; one whose posting window had already ended before setup still counts as expected, late, in the walk and never "not seen yet", until it is seen or the person says it will not come with a `skip` [Z2], notyet_window_past.json; such an item is settled by a matching import line at any later date, its posting window not applying until it is settled [AA14], notyet_import_late.json), is on the check-in list until the account's first check-in after setup, and an import that settles it stays outside the opening (section 7.1). **A line that settles a `notYet` occurrence comes after the opening, whatever its date** [AB4]: a statement line carrying only the bill's own date, or a typed line dated before the setup day, still lowers the account from the opening (notyet_line_before.json). Every other item due or landing on or before the opening is inside it.

**Before the opening** [B7, H1]. An event dated before an account's opening day, or on that day and told before the opening, does not move that account (the opening figure holds it). An event on the opening day told after setup (a dinner logged that evening) moves it like any later event. `pre` is worked out each time from the dates, never stored. An event between two accounts where one side is before its opening: the other side's amount goes to Waiting, so the accounts still add up to the places. Pre-opening spends do not count in that period's "spent so far" for budget lines and Free to spend: the opening split already holds what was left [T watch, corrected; the reference model always did this].

## 4. Places
The ledger's places: Waiting, Bills left (with one "for the card" line per card paid in full), Free to spend, and the kept places.
```
{ id, name, kind: 'goal'|'emergency'|'education'|'giving'|'investing'|'payoff',
  target, due, cur, owner,
  keptIn: { acct, mode: 'inBank'|'ownAccount' },
  pays: <account id>,          // kind payoff: the debt it pays down; its money is never opened as savings (no opening, no split; a moved one's 38.31 "saved so far" is the loan's `paidBefore` [Z6]); money set aside in it is held like any place and is not a payment (payoff_place.json)
  emergency, stretch, stretchOn, stretchFrom,   // the emergency place, as 38.31
  slot, why,                   // 38.31 screen fields, kept
  refill: true,                // a yearly cost: after each payment the set-aside starts again for the next one [E5]
  gone, at }
```
- Education, Giving and Investing are places with `keptIn` like goals and are protected in the headroom [B10].
- A place's balance is added up from events (`open`, `move`, `spend` from it, `adjust` naming it) and the moves of period-end answer records (section 7.4) [P2, Q watch item]; never typed. **A move out of a place** lowers it [T7]: a `move` with `place` (the place it leaves) and `place2` (where it goes; none: Waiting). "Saved so far" typed at setup becomes part of the bank's opening split.
- **Paying from a place** [H7]: a spend with `place`, or a bill whose schedule has `place`, takes its money from that place first. In the walk, a future bill paid from a place comes off only beyond what the place still holds, so the money is never taken off twice. A place never holds less than nothing: paying past it takes the rest from the account's other money.
- **Refill** [E5, H7]: a place with `refill: true` that pays a schedule is, after each payment, due again on that schedule's next occurrence; what it held after paying is where the set-aside starts again.
- `mode 'inBank'` (the default): the money stays in that account, set aside. `'ownAccount'`: the place is backed by its own account (`spendFrom: false`). **Moving a place's money to its own account** [V1]: the account must be in the home currency in force (another currency is stage 2) [W3]; the move writes a `transfer` of the money and sets `keptIn` to that account with mode `ownAccount`; the place is then counted at that account (held in its walk), and the account it left holds it no longer and gets no warning for it. A transfer moves account money, not the place's balance, and the opening split stays on the account it was typed on.
- **A place removed with money in it** [O] first writes a `move` of what it holds to Waiting, then is marked `gone`; the money is never left in no place. **To place** [P1]: that move carries `toPlace: true` and has the built id `rm:<place>` [Q7]. The money it moved stays out of safe to spend (held in the walk like a kept place, its `why` line "<name>'s money, in Waiting to place") until the person places it. Placing writes a `move` out of Waiting into a place with `placed: <id of the toPlace move>`; placings on two devices that add up to more than was to place are checked again after a merge, and the newest gives way [X5]; what is still to place is the `toPlace` move's amount less the moves that name it, and placing moves the money, so the headline does not change. A move out of Waiting that names no `toPlace` move comes from Waiting's other money. **Where it may go** [R1]: into a place (above), into Free to spend, where it counts in safe to spend again (Free to spend's money this period grows by it), or kept in Waiting on purpose, a `move` with `placed` whose place is Waiting itself, where it is no longer to place and still does not count in safe to spend.

## 5. Schedules (pay, bills, transfers, spending that logs itself)
```
{ id, kind: 'income'|'bill'|'transfer'|'autoSpend'|'once',
  name, note, acct, acct2,    // note: a moved one-off's 38.31 note [AA2]; acct: where it lands or is paid from; acct2: the other side of a transfer
  owner, cur,
  rules: [ { from: '2026-11-20', rule, at } ],               // [H2] each rule holds from its date to the next one's; each entry has its own `at` [L merge q2]
  // rule: { every: 'month', days: [10] }                     // days of the month; 31 = last day
  //     | { every: 'weeks', n: 2, from: '2026-11-13' }       // every N weeks from a date (weekly: n 1)
  //     | { every: 'months', n: 3, day: 5, from: '2026-12-05' }
  //     | { every: 'monthsList', months: [6, 11], day: 15 }
  //     | { every: 'year', date: '05-31' }
  //     | { every: 'lastWorkday' }                           // the month's last working day; never moved
  //     | { every: 'once', date: '2027-03-14' }
  //     | { every: 'none' }                                  // no fixed day: no future money expected [B4]
  shift: 'before'|'after'|'none',
  steps: [ { from: '2026-11-23', amount: 250000, at } ],      // the usual amount, changing from a date (a raise); its own `at` [L merge q2]
  per: 'pay'|'month',                                          // income and bills [AB2, AC1, AD4]: 'pay' (the default) each step is one occurrence's amount; 'month' each step is a monthly total, each occurrence getting monthly x 12 / the occurrences in that year (units rule, H9); moved pay is 'month', and a moved bill answered with another rhythm than once a month
  byDay: { '15': 18000, '30': 12000 },                         // unequal halves on different days
  amounts: { '2027-06-10': 260000 },                           // one occurrence changed, keyed by the due date BEFORE any move [B8]; it wins over `steps` (one_occurrence_amount.json)
  vary: 0.3,                                                   // the amount margin for matching, for example power and water [A6]; none: the exact amount only [L matching q1]
  startsPeriod: true,                                          // income only; see below
  place: 'bills'|'free'|<place id>,                            // where a bill is paid from
  fee: 1500,                                                   // transfer only: what each occurrence costs on top of the amount [Q2]
  roughAll: true,                                              // the "all my bills, roughly" line, split later [A8]
  from: '2026-11-23', ends: null, count: null,                 // first occurrence, last date, or a number of occurrences; closing its account sets `ends` to the closing day [P6]
  redirect: <place id>,                                        // where the money goes after `ends` (38.31 line redirect): planned money, never counted until moved [F22]; nothing is due after `ends` (bill_ends_redirect.json)
  gone, at }
```
- **Rules from a date** [H2]. A change to when something happens (pay moves from the 10th to the 25th) adds a `rules` entry from a date; it never edits an older entry, so no occurrence before that date is rewritten or made up. An occurrence due on or after an entry's `from`, and before the next entry's, comes from that entry. A change to how much adds a `steps` entry the same way. **Never on or before what is settled or named** [X8]: a change can never start on or before its schedule's latest settled or named occurrence (a settle, a `once` or `amounts` key, a `pe:` or `ps:` record); the edit offers the first day after that occurrence's due date (`rules/edits.json`), so a settle or a name never points at an occurrence the schedule no longer makes. The reference model refuses a copy where one does (c8).
- **Occurrence names** [H2]. An occurrence is named everywhere by its schedule and its due date before any move: `{ s: <schedule id>, due: '2027-01-10' }`, written `pay1@2027-01-10` on screens and in tests (it lands on Fri 8 Jan). Settled links, `amounts` keys, expected item ids and the check-in list all use this one name. A5's sequence number is dropped. The edit screen lists the settled occurrences that the new entry would make again before it saves.
- **One stream starts periods** [F12]. At most one income schedule has `startsPeriod: true`. A couple picks it; the default is the first entered. Others (a second job, a partner's pay) are counted in the walk but start no period.
- **Pay is never blank** [A8]: setup asks for the next pay date and assumes monthly when nothing else is said. `every: 'none'` is chosen only by a person who says there is no fixed day.
- **transfer** moves money between two own accounts on a day (a top-up from the bank to an e-wallet); **autoSpend** writes its own spend event on the day it lands, with `settles` naming its occurrence, unless something already settled it (38.31 `plan.recurring`). Its id is `auto:<schedule>@<due>` [O2], so two devices writing it make one record. Its `told` is when it was written (the next time the app was opened); dated on a check's day or the setup day, it counts as inside that figure [O1]. It is never on the check-in list. Both are in the walk [B5, H7]. A transfer between two currencies carries `a` and `a2` [H5].
- **A bill or a self-logged spend paid by card** [T3] (a schedule whose `acct` is a card): each occurrence still to come is a future card line on its landing day. It is never in what the card owes now; in the look ahead it comes off the card's paying account on the payment day of the statement it falls in (close day not sure: the first payment day on or after it), with the open statement's payment, or on its own for a later statement inside the walk.
- **A transfer schedule's `fee`** [Q2]: each occurrence takes the amount plus the fee off the paying account and brings the amount to the other; the check-in item asks the amount plus the fee, and ticking it writes `a` (what left) and `a2` (what arrived), and the fee record (section 6.2) [S5].
- **Pay in another currency** [Q4]: an income schedule whose `cur` differs from its account's (dollars paid into a yen bank, yen converted into a peso bank) is in its own currency. Until stage 2 it is not counted in the walk (it cannot be converted, F11) and Home shows a warning under the headline; it still starts the period and is still expected and named as usual. When it arrives, the `in` carries `a` (what arrived, in the account's currency) and `a2`, the original amount in the schedule's currency. **In the lists** [S6]: it is left out of the "not seen yet" totals (still listed there, in its own currency), and the check-in lists it apart, in its own currency, outside the list's total.
- **Removing a self-logged spend** [Q5] (a parent paid the gym this month) marks the `auto:` record `gone` and writes `skip:<occurrence>`, so the occurrence is "did not happen" and is not logged again on any device. A self-logged spend of an occurrence that has a live skip does not count, so a second device's own `auto:` copy that comes back in a merge changes nothing (final_review_2 S2; the Q5 reading, confirmed in S).
- **Holiday and bank-closed tables** are data files per country and year [F17]: holidays and bank-closed days are separate tables, both non-working for the shift rule. A missing year falls back to weekends only and the screen says so.

## 6. Events (what really happened)

### 6.1 Fields
```
{ id, k, sub, date, a, a2, acct, acct2, place, place2,
  line,                      // the budget line a spend is for
  stream,                    // income: the schedule it came from
  settles: [ { s, due } ],   // the occurrences it settles, by name [H2] (one payment can settle two)
  posted,                    // the posting date from the bank or a statement, beside `date` (the buy date) [H1]; on a two-sided record, the side money leaves [Q1]
  posted2,                   // a two-sided record only: the posting date of the side money reaches (a top-up seen on the e-wallet's statement days later) [Q1]
  told,                      // when the app first heard of it; never changed (section 0) [H1]
  est: true,                 // typed as rough [A6]; on a two-sided record, each side is rough until a line meets it [S1]
  src: { type: 'typed'|'statement'|'screenshot'|'move', file, line, from, to, bal },   // [A6]; a statement's `from` and `to` are the dates it covers [L matching q8]; `bal` the running balance printed after the line, when there is one [O1]; on a two-sided record, the source of the side money leaves [Q1]
  src2,                      // a two-sided record only: the source of the side money reaches, the same shape as `src` [Q1]
  notDouble: [ <event id> ],  // the person said "both are real": never offered as a possible double with these; on the newer record [L matching q12]
  grp,                       // the check-in (section 7) or the one step it belongs to
  owner,                     // only when different from the account's owner
  held: { came, at },        // only on a copy held on arrival: the `at` it came with and the `at` it was held at [N1] (section 0)
  toPlace: true,             // on the `move` to Waiting written when a place with money is removed: that money is "to place" [P1] (section 4)
  placed,                    // on a `move` out of Waiting that places money to place: the id of the `toPlace` move [P1]
  of,                        // on a fee's `spend`: the id of the transfer it belongs to [S5] (section 6.2)
  place,                     // on a `filler`: where it comes out of, Free to spend by default [T2]; on a `move` out of a place, the place it leaves [T7]
  note, gone, goneBy, at }
```

### 6.2 Kinds (`k`)
The ledger's kinds and its one rule stand: `open`, `in`, `borrow`, `move`, `spend`, `hand`, `transfer`, `convert`, `repay`, `check`, `adjust`. Added:
- `skip`: an occurrence that did not happen this time (no pay this month, a bill waived, a self-logged spend someone else paid). It carries `settles`. Written by removing a self-logged spend, its id is `skip:<occurrence>` [Q5] (section 5).
- `periodStart` [N5, T1]: a new period the person started without the pay (section 7.4): `{ k: 'periodStart', date }`, id `ps:<the occurrence of the pay it stands in for>`, written by the person's choice at the check-in, with the usual `told`, `at` and `by`. Its `date` is the day they chose; two phones that start it on different days make one record and the newer `at` wins. It moves no money. Undo marks it `gone` like any record, and then it starts nothing.
- `filler`: the rest of the spending in a stretch (section 7.3). It carries `stretch: { from, to }`, `place`, `split: 'days'|'before'` and **no stored amount** [A3]. **It is spending** [T2]: it comes out of Free to spend for the period or periods its stretch covers (split as A4), or out of the place or budget line the person picks at the check-in (`place`).
- A check carries `typedA` and, on a bank, `avail: 'available'|'current'`; on a card, `stmt: true` with `stmtDate` when it is the statement figure, and at the opening `stmtPay` (its payment day) when it is one of several unpaid statements [J5]. An "available" check already has pending debits off, so it holds every event dated before its day, posted or not, and those on its day told before it; a "current" check follows the posted dates (section 7.1). Not asked: "current" [I1, J3].
- `a2` [H5, Q2, Q4]: on a `repay`, `transfer`, `borrow` or `convert`, `a` is what left (in the paying account's currency) and `a2` what arrived. `a2` is kept whenever what arrived differs from what left, in any currency; it is left out only when they are the same. Between two accounts in one currency the difference is a **fee** [Q2], so a top-up of 1,000.00 that costs 1,015.00 leaves the e-wallet at 1,000.00 and takes 15.00 from Free to spend (final_review_2 S4). **The fee record** [S5]: a fee is its own `spend` linked to its transfer, `{ id: 'fee:<transfer id>', k: 'spend', acct: <sending account>, a: <a less a2>, line: 'fees', place, of: <transfer id> }` [X2], with the place the person picked (a kept place), Free to spend by default (no `place`). It moves no money of its own: **the transfer's `a` is what left, the fee included, and the fee record moves only the place, never the account** [S5 fix], so the sending account goes down by the transfer's `a` once, and the record says which place pays the fee. It counts only while its transfer is live (with its transfer gone it counts nowhere). On an `in` from a schedule in another currency, `a2` is the original amount in that currency [Q4].
- `adjust` stays only for a person's explicit "accept the difference" outside a check-in (rare). A check-in never writes an adjust. **What it moves** [T7, LEDGER_DESIGN]: its account by its signed `a`, and Free to spend, or the place it names (`place`), by the same.
- `hand` (Wallet mode) is **stage 2** [T7]: the field stays and is kept on load, but version one has no screens for it; Mako uses Track.
- `borrow` [O5, Q3]: cash taken on a card, or money drawn on a loan or pay-later account (`acct` the card or loan, `acct2` where the money went). It and a `transfer` out of a card are card lines (section 3); on a loan or pay-later account it raises what is owed.
- **A period-end answer** [P2, Q watch item, T1] (`k: 'periodEnd'`) is not a set of `move` events: it is one record per place, `pe:<the occurrence that started the period>:<place>`, dated the period's last day, holding all that place's moves, `{ place, moves: [ { to, a } ], told, at, by }` (section 7.4). Place balances read its moves as they read a `move`: a move to a place fills it, and the place it answers for gives what it moves elsewhere. (This replaces the "move events" of the earlier text.)
- An `in` that is a refund may carry `line` [O]: only when the person picks the budget line it goes back to; it then gives back to that line's spent so far. A refund with no `line` lowers the card or raises the account and gives nothing back to any line (the safe side).

### 6.3 Receipts are spend events
One list. A 38.31 receipt moves in as `{ k: 'spend', date: r.d, a, acct, line or place, src: { type: 'move', id: r.id }, note }` (section 11).

### 6.4 Matching an import to what is already there [A6, F15, L]
- **What matches.** An import line matches a typed line or an expected occurrence by account, direction (money out with money out), amount and date. Rules file: `rules/matching.json`.
- **Amount.** A typed line: exactly, or when it is `est`, within 10% of the larger amount, |a - b| x 10 <= the larger, compared exactly in minor units [H9, L q10]. An expected occurrence: within its schedule's `vary`; no `vary` is the exact amount, and a different real amount is ticked by the person at the check-in [A6, L q1].
- **Date.** A typed line matches an import line posted inside its account's posting window either side of the typed day (`postingDays` working days before to the same after) [F4, L q3]. A bank line goes by `posted` when there is one; a card line by its buy date or its posted date, and a card's window is 7 working days by default [L q4, M1]. An expected occurrence is settled by an import landing from 5 working days before its landing day to the end of its posting window after it [L q2]; a `notYet` item whose window ended before setup, at any later date [AA14].
- **Which one.** A typed line is tried before an expected occurrence: the typed line wins, is replaced (`gone`, `goneBy: <import event id>`), and the import takes over its `settles` [L q5, q6] and its `told` [O1], so a check that held the typed line holds the import. Several match: the closest date wins, then the earliest `told` [L q7].
- **Rank.** Statement over screenshot over typed (`src.type`). A statement removes every screenshot line inside its own dates (`src.from` to `src.to`), matched or not, `goneBy` the statement import's id [A6, L q8]. A screenshot read after the statement, inside its dates, that matches no statement line is removed too, `goneBy` the statement import; one that matches a statement line goes `goneBy` that line [M2]. An import line of equal rank that repeats a live line is dropped, never added: the same line id (date, amount, direction and its count among such lines in its own import) or the same `src` (a file read twice) [L q9].
- **Possible double** [H9, T watch]. A transfer typed on two devices (the same kind, the same two accounts, the same amount, the same day) is offered like a spend, never against a spend. The same amount typed on two devices on the same day (the spend's date [L q13]) for the same place (a budget line counts as the place [L q11]) is offered to the person; nothing is removed without them. "Both are real" is kept as `notDouble: [id]` on the newer record, and the pair is not offered again [L q12].
- **Two-sided records** [Q1]. An import line that meets one side of a two-sided record (`transfer`, `repay`, `borrow`, `convert`) attaches to it and never replaces it: a money-out line on the account money leaves meets that side by `a`; a money-in line on the account it reaches meets that side by `a2` (else `a`), inside that account's posting window either side of the record's date. It sets that side's posted date and source (`posted` and `src` for the side it leaves, `posted2` and `src2` for the side it reaches) and is not a record of its own, so a statement read after typing ATM cash as a transfer, or after ticking a card payment, takes the money off once (final_review_2 S1). A typed line is tried first, then a two-sided record, then an expected occurrence; several records fit: the closest date, then the earliest `told`. A line read as a two-sided record itself (a reader that knows it is the card company) meets the side of the account whose statement it came from. A repeat of an attached line (the same statement read twice) is dropped like any equal-rank repeat [L q9]. Each side's posted date counts for that side's checks (section 7.1). **A rough record** (`est`) [S1]: a side is met by a line within 10% of that side's amount (|a - b| x 10 <= the larger, as H9) and takes the line's amount; the other side keeps its typed amount, and in one currency the difference is a fee on the sender [Q2], written as its fee record [S5]. A record that is not rough is met only by each side's own amount; a line further than 10% is a record of its own. **More arriving than left** [U1]: when the line would make more arrive than left in one currency (a typed 10,000 to cash, the bank line 9,800; an e-wallet line in above the typed top-up), both sides take the line's amount unless the other side has its own line; nothing arrives that did not leave, and no fee is written.
- **The nearest line settles** [Q6]. An import line that matches no typed line and no two-sided record may settle an expected occurrence (above). Across one import, each unsettled occurrence (in landing order) is settled by the line nearest its landing day, then the line nearest its amount, then the first listed, so a returned deposit three days before pay does not settle the pay when the pay's own line is in the same file (final_review_2 S8). A nearer line read later moves the settle to it, and the period start with it, whether it comes in the same check-in stretch [Q6] or after a check-in [S2]; `settles` is saved at import time and moved by that later import. **A ticked settle stays** [S2]: when the person ticked the settle at a check-in (the check-in's `settled` names the line), a nearer line read later does not move it. A settle held by a typed record (a ticked `set:` record) stays as before. A line that settles nothing and matches nothing is a new line.

## 7. The check-in, settling and the filler [A1 to A4, F15, F16, F24]

### 7.1 One moment
```
{ id, date, told, at,
  figures: [ { acct, check: <event id>, typedA } ],   // each account typed, each with its own date; a check with a large gain carries `gain` (section 7.3)
  settled: [ <event id>, ... ],                       // what the ticked list wrote, and imported lines whose settle the person ticked [S2]
  fillers: [ <event id>, ... ],
  gone, at }
```
The checks, settlements and fillers written together carry `grp: <checkin id>`. Each account's bank figure carries its own "as of" date (its latest check); safe to spend reports its oldest input [A1]. After a gap the bank figure stays at the last check, shown with its age; nothing is projected as fact [F24].

**What a check holds** [H1]. A check holds the events dated before its day, and those dated on its day whose `told` is before the check's `told`. A spend logged after a same-day check (lunch after the morning check-in) comes after it. A bank event with `posted` is held by its posted date, so a debit bought on the 21st and posted on the 24th is not inside a check on the 22nd and still comes off. Each side of a two-sided record goes by its own posted date, `posted` on the side money leaves and `posted2` on the side it reaches [Q1], so a top-up typed on Friday that reaches the e-wallet on Monday is not inside the e-wallet's Saturday check. That is a "current" check. An "available" one already has pending debits off, so it holds every event dated before its day by that date, posted or not, and on its day those told before it [I1, J3]. A spend told after a check comes after it, whatever the check kind [J3]. Two check-ins on one day: the later one's stretch starts at the earlier one.

**Lines the app hears of late** [O1]. An import that replaces a typed line keeps that line's `told`. An import line that replaces no typed line, and a spend that logs itself written late, dated on a check's day (by the day the check goes by: posted for a current check) or on the setup day, counts as inside that figure, whatever its `told`: so a statement read a week after a check-in shrinks that check's filler instead of lowering the bank. When the statement prints a running balance (`src.bal`), it decides instead: the lines of that day up to the one whose balance is the check's figure are inside the check, the rest after it (when the balance before the day's first line is the figure, none is inside). An import that settles an item in the opening's `notYet` stays outside the opening [F20, X3].

### 7.2 Settling first [A2, F16]
The check-in first lists every expected item (pay, card payments, bills, transfers) that landed since the last check-in and is not settled, ticked by default, amount editable. Ticking writes the event, with the id `set:<occurrence>` [O2], so two people ticking the same pay on two phones make one record (the newest `at` wins): pay writes `in` with `stream` and `settles` (that starts the period); a card payment writes `repay`; a bill writes `spend` with `settles`. A different real amount settles the occurrence at the real amount and does not change later estimates unless the person ticks "use this from now on", which writes a new `steps` entry [F16]. The reference model's `checkIn.toConfirm` is that list. An item in the opening's `notYet` is on it too while its account has had no check-in since setup, though it landed on or before the opening [X3]. A card, loan or pay-later payment in another currency is listed apart, in its own currency, outside the total [W2]. Pay in another currency than its account is listed apart, in its own currency, and is not in the list's total (`checkIn.apart`) [S6]. A settle the person ticks at a check-in stays even when a nearer line is read later (section 6.4) [S2]. **Already matched** [U2]: the settles imports made since the last check-in are listed as "already matched", ticked; saving the check-in names them in its `settled`, so they stay [S2]. The person can untick one: that line then settles nothing, and its item is back on the list to confirm (the model's `checkIn.matched`; wording for Mako's copy pass).

### 7.3 The filler [A3, A4, F15]
What is left after settling is the filler for the stretch from the day after the last check-in to this one (from the same day, when the last check-in was earlier that day [H1]). Its amount is never stored: it is worked out each time as (what the events say the account holds at the check) minus (the check figure). Lines imported or typed later inside the stretch shrink it (section 7.1, O1). A stretch that crosses a period start is split by days, or all to the older period when the person says "mostly before pay" [A4]. **The filler is spending** [T2]: each part comes out of Free to spend for its period, or out of the place or budget line the person picks at the check-in. It is signed: a filler below zero (a pending hold that dropped, a spend logged twice) gives back, so a hold and its return add up to nothing; a large one is asked about first (below). A card check's filler counts the same way; an investment's gap is never a filler. On a card, a check that names a statement fills that statement's cycle first, then the open part. Reference: `makeFiller` and the card check in `cardInfo`.

**A large gain is asked about** [O, X4]. When a check-in figure is above what the app had for the account by more than the period's Free to spend, or by more than 20% of what the app had (a bank figure typed into the e-wallet's box), the check-in asks whether the figure is right. The check saves the question's state, `gain: 'asked' | 'right' | 'wrong'` [X4]. The gain is held, not placed in any place (Free to spend, a budget line or a goal), until the person answers that the figure is right; a check with no answer counts as asked. **Answered wrong** [Z1]: the check is removed (marked `gone`), it counts nowhere, and the account asks for the figure again (`check_again`) until a later check is typed (gain_wrong.json). The figure stands as the account's bank figure meanwhile. An investment's rise is never asked [T watch]. Reference: `heldGain` and the `gain_to_ask` warning (gain_held.json, gain_answered.json, gain_pay_day.json).

### 7.4 Period end [A7]
The period-end question (always asked) comes when the next period-starting pay is seen [L period_end q2], and runs after a fresh check-in: if none is dated in the last 3 days of the period or after its end (whose stretch is split by days at the pay, A4) [L q1], the question starts with one. A check-in is fresh only when every spend-from account was typed [L q9]; the check-ins of the last 3 days count together, so the bank typed on one day and the cash on another is fresh [M6]. While pay is late the period waits for it; after its posting window the check-in asks "did your pay come?", and a yes with its day starts the new period [L q3]. **Periods are named by their pay** [T1]: a period is named by the occurrence of the pay that starts it (`pay@2026-12-10`), never by a day that can move. A new period the person starts without the pay is saved as a `periodStart` event dated the day they chose, id `ps:<the occurrence it stands in for>` [N5, O2, T1]; the new period starts on that day, and the old one ends the day before; the late pay stays expected until its posting window ends, then is "not seen yet" [M7]. Only the `periodStart` for the next pay's occurrence ends a period; one for the pay that began it does not. **Once a period's end is fixed** (by a period start or by answers), the late pay settles its occurrence but starts no new period, and a moved settle (Q6, S2), a corrected pay date or an undone pay never moves a period that already has answers [T1]. Money left in Free to spend or a budget line never rolls on silently: it waits in its place marked "to decide" until answered, and "to decide" money is not in the new period's safe to spend [L q7]. Answers are not `move` events: **one record per place** [P2, Q watch item], dated the period's last day: a place's answer is saved as one record, id `pe:<the occurrence that started the period>:<place>` [O2, T1], holding all its moves, `{ place, moves: [ { to, a } ], told, at, by }`; a split leftover (part to a goal, part kept, part to Waiting) is several moves in that one record, a move to the place itself is "keep it here", and an overspent line's record holds its covers as `{ from, a }` (a cover is saved under the line it covers). So the same place answered on two phones is one record, and the merge keeps the newer record whole, never mixing moves from two. **Two answers of one period** (different places, on one phone or two) share its occurrence name, so they answer one period; when they are dated different last days, the earliest last day stands as the period's end [V3]. The refusal below is checked again on the merged answers [O2]. **After a merge** [P3, made general by X5: every share-out from a limited amount, placing money to place (section 4), covering an overspend found later and period-end answers]: when answers of different places add up to more than is left, the answer with the newer `at` among those giving from that place gives way: its moves are undone (the record is marked `gone`, `goneBy` the answer kept), and the next open of the device that wrote it says "An answer from your other device was used. Check it." Repeated until nothing is refused; answers that take nothing from that place stay, however new; "keep it here" writes no move, the money stays in its place and the next period's amount adds on top [L q6]; answers adding up to more than is left are refused, with the amount left shown [L q10]; **an answer the move wrote** (its record's `src` names the 38.31 field) is never refused: it is capped at what its place still holds, and the rest of the place waits "to decide" [AB watch] (`rules/period_end.json` pe-62 to pe-65); one naming a goal the plan no longer has moves to Waiting as "to place" (`toPlace: true`) [AC8] (pe-66). A moved answer still waiting for this period (`pendingMove`, AB2) is offered as the default, capped the same way; the person confirms or changes it, and what they confirm is their own answer [AD3] (pe-67 to pe-70). An overspent line is shown with how much it is over and is covered from what is left in Free to spend, then Waiting, as the person picks (moves into it) [L q4]. With nothing left anywhere the screen shows one line, "nothing left this time", and asks nothing [L q5]. Two unanswered period ends stay apart, each answered as its own period [L q8, T1]. **Found later** [O, P5]: an old statement imported after a period-end answer leaves the answer as it is; its spending dated in that period comes out of what still waits to decide first, and never out of the money kept for the next period; what the answers gave beyond what was really left is shown at the next check-in as an overspend "found later". There the person picks where it comes from: Waiting or this period's Free to spend, in any split. Each pick is a `move` from Waiting or Free to spend into the place, dated the check-in day. A pick from anywhere else (the kept money included), or picks adding up to more than was found later, are refused. Rules file: `rules/period_end.json`.

## 8. The plan (what is expected)
- **`plan.shares`**: `[ { id, from, cur, acct, per: { free: 'month', edu: 'month', ... }, free, edu, give, ffa, goals: { <place id>: amount }, once: { <occurrence>: { free, edu, give, ffa, goals: { <place id>: amount } } }, at } ]`. Each entry holds from its date to the next. `once` is a one-period override, keyed like a budget line's by the occurrence of the pay that starts the period, and holds only the amounts it changes (a moved 38.31 one-month share or goal amount, on the entry in force on that occurrence's due day) [AA6, T1]. `per` is per amount [B8]. `cur` is the currency of its amounts [O6]: a change of home currency starts a new entry, and the older entries keep their own. `acct` is the account Free to spend is held in (default `home.mainBank`) [O4]. A change never moves anything dated before it, and applies from the next period start [T watch] (the period running when it is made keeps its amounts); a change dated on a period's first day applies to that period [V].
- **`plan.budgets`** (budget lines: food, transport, other card spending, custom lines): `[ { id, name, place: 'bills'|'free'|<place id>, cur, acct, per: 'month'|'pay'|'week', steps: [ { from, amount } ], once: { <period start date>: amount }, at } ]`. `place` may be any place, so 38.31 custom lines in Education, Giving or Investing keep their home [B8]. `once` is a one-period override, keyed by the occurrence of the pay that starts the period (`{ 'pay@2027-01-10': amount }`), never by a start day, which is not known until the pay lands [B8, T1]. A spend carries `line`. **Currency** [T5]: a line's spent so far counts only spends in its own currency; a spend in another currency on a home line is listed apart with a warning (`spend_other_currency`) until stage 2. **Overspent** [T4]: in the look ahead an overspent line counts as nothing left; the overspend has already left the account.
- **Which account a line is on** [O4]. `acct` on a budget line and on the Free to spend share says which account its money is held in; left out, `home.mainBank`. Spent so far counts a line's spends on every account (food bought on the card counts against Food). What is left of a line is reserved from its account; a line on a card is reserved from the card's paying account, and the spends already on the card come off with the card's payment, so the money is counted once (final_review_1 finding 4: the line on the bank took food off twice, the line on the card never). An opening split for a card's line is part of its paying account's split.
- **Units** [10.4, H9, L]: a period gets the amount when `per` matches its kind; `month` with pay that is not monthly gets amount x 12 / the real number of pays in that year (26 or 27 for pay every two weeks, 52 or 53 weekly), so the year adds up exactly, the leftover minor units going to the earliest pays of the year [L units q4]. The count is by the unshifted due date's year, and a period that crosses 31 Dec uses its start year [L q3]; a period gets the amount of the pay that starts it: that pay's place in its own year's list, the year by its unshifted due date [N3], so a period that starts on 31 Dec with its pay due 1 Jan (moved earlier) gets the next year's first amount (N3 replaces M8 here); a schedule's first year counts by its rhythm [L q5]; a schedule whose rule changes during the year (pay every two weeks, then monthly) counts each due date by the rule in force on it [O], and **each rule gets its own months** [P4]: a monthly amount covers the months each rule is in force in that year (the first rule from 1 Jan), spread over that rule's pays, the leftover minor units on that rule's earliest pays, so the year adds up to 12 months (two-weekly January to June: 6 x 50,000 = 300,000 over 13 pays, 23,077 or 23,076; monthly July to December: 50,000 each; 600,000 in the year). **A rule that changes on a day other than the 1st** [R2]: that month goes to the rule in force on its first day, and the new rule's months start the next month; its pays still follow the new rule, so every due date from its `from` is one of its pays, a pay in the month of the change included (two-weekly to Thu 15 Jul, then monthly on the 25th: January to July, 350,000 over the 14 two-weekly pays to 2 Jul, 25,000 each; August to December, 250,000 over the 6 monthly pays 25 Jul to 25 Dec, 41,667 or 41,666; units-70 to units-74). N3's "the pay that starts it" means that pay's due-date year [O, O-confirm]: units-50 is 11,556. `week` gets days x amount / 7, rounded down per period; weekly amounts are a rate and need not add up to a year [L q6]. With no fixed pay, budget lines and Free to spend come off by day: the monthly amount x 12 / the year's days (365 or 366) a day, rounded down, with the remainder on the month's last day, so each month comes to its own share rounded down and nothing carries to the next month [M10]; `per: week` gives a seventh of the week's amount each day, rounded down, with the remainder on the week's last day [M9], and a week ends on the seventh day counted from when the line started (the start is day 1), not on a fixed weekday [N4]; `per: pay` means each money-in event [B4, L q7]. The reference model keeps a plain form (52 / n pays a year, each period rounded on its own); the engine uses the units rules [L q8]. Units have their own test file (section 12).
- **`plan.limits`**: `{ ceiling, aims, cur, at }` (38.31 `cfg.ceiling`, `cfg.aims`); `cur` is the currency of its amounts [O6].
- **Opening mid-period** [B2, I4]: the opening asks "spent since pay day?" for Free to spend and each budget line, or shares them by days left, and writes those as the places' opening split. Days left: from the setup day to the period's last day, both counted, over the period's days; each line rounded down to the minor unit; Free to spend is its own share plus the lines' rounding leftovers, then rounded down, and a total under one minor unit stays in Waiting [confirmed in J]. When the person typed Free to spend, that figure stands and the lines' rounding leftovers stay in Waiting [J4].
- **Planned goal set-asides are not money yet** [F22]: only `move` events into a place count.

## 9. Periods (a view, never stored)
A period is named by the occurrence of the period stream's pay that starts it [T1]. It runs from the day that pay lands (early or late) [F8], or from the date of the `periodStart` that stands in for it [N5], or, when the period before has period-end answers, from the day after their last day [T1], to the day before the next one. Period starts are in the reference model [T6]. **Late pay** [H8]: a pay past its date but inside its account's posting window is "late, expected". It still counts in the walk (on today), the period does not move on, and days to next pay says the pay is late. After the window it becomes "not seen yet" and stops counting. Its name on Home is its dates. When the person starts the new period without it, a `periodStart` event is saved and the period starts on its date [N5]; the pay keeps the same states, late and expected until the window ends, then not seen yet [M7]. With no fixed pay the period runs from the last money in, and the engine gives "lasts about N weeks" [B4]. Changing the pay schedule moves no event; it only regroups them.

## 11. Moving a saved copy [E1, E4, E6]
The move is proved on every old build Mako's data can be in: 38.28 live and every build from 25.0 to 38.28, not only 38.31 [E1]. Every typed figure, receipt and goal is accounted for, and a test lists any field it could not place. The bank comparison Mako sees before deploy is redone on the new engine. The any-AI paste format and its checker write this shape [E4].

The move reads the raw stored text (`frozen3831`, kept as the device's `mv:frozen:<by>` record, section 2.1), never a copy that a build's clean-up has already rewritten [O].

**Questions the move asks once** (until answered, a banner asks and the engine uses the safe side): the currency code when the symbol is "$", "¥" or unknown, when the copy has no currency at all, or when it holds ₱ with preset `legacy` or no preset (38.28's clean-up writes ₱ and `legacy` into any copy without a currency, so that ₱ is not a choice) [O] (asked before any amount is converted to minor units; never set to pesos [H9]; `rules/units.json` units-63 to units-66); the bank figure, when the copy has none (at the first check-in; safe to spend shows nothing until then) [Y19]; when each pay comes in, and its rhythm: 38.31 pay was monthly [AA4] (until answered, no future pay is counted, and a one-month amount or month-end answer waits in `pendingMove`, section 2.1 [AA5]); when each bill is due (until answered, due on the period's first day, and its one-month amounts wait in `pendingMove`); which loan a "Loan or debt payment" line is, when the copy has debts [E6] (with none, the line is a loan account of its own whose balance is asked [Y4]); each debt's payment day [Y9] (until answered, its one-month amounts wait in `pendingMove`); the minimum, "Keep at least ___ in your bank?" with the box empty, when the move drops one as automatic [AD1]; the balance of each loan the move makes [Y4, Y11], and its yearly rate and minimum ("don't know": rate 0 and the old monthly payment as a fixed minimum) [AA12]; until answered it holds the same, marked `terms: 'asked'` [AC3]; where each goal's money is, for every goal with money, in the bank or in another account the person names, a bank or an e-wallet as they say [Y17, AA13] (in another account: that account is opened with it and the goal is kept there as its own account, in the home currency [W3], goal_money_elsewhere.json; until answered, in the bank, the safe side, and a bank holding less than the goal is shown as short, never refused [Z3], goal_money_unanswered.json). Every question is recorded with its answer. Every record the move writes has the id `mv:<kind>:<38.31 id or key>` [X1], so running the move on two devices makes one copy.

**The move on a second device, later** [AB1]. A moved record carries the copy's last save as `at` (AA10), so any real edit made after the move wins at the merge. A device that finds a moved copy in the cloud takes it and adds only the ids it lacks (its own frozen text, a receipt its older copy held that the other's did not); it never overwrites. A device that moves offline meets the other copy in the merge, and the week of real use on the first device still stands (`rules/move.json` mv-82 to mv-85, mv-97 to mv-99). Each device keeps its own raw text as `mv:frozen:<by>` (section 2.1).

### 11.1 Every 38.31 field and where it goes
Ids [X1, Y28, Z]: `mv:<kind>:<38.31 id, key or field path>`, kinds as MOVE_FIELDS.md section 7. "From a month" is from that month's first day [Y3]: a step is an entry from its month's first day; the amount in force in the opening month is the first entry, from the opening; steps before the opening stay only in `frozen3831`. A line or pay amount that is 0 in every month is not moved and is listed in the move summary [Y25]. An amount with more decimals than its currency is rounded half up and flagged [Y23]. The copy's last save is the later of the IndexedDB second copy's `t` and the stored `rev` when it is a time, either one alone, else the move's own time [Y13, AA10]. With the sample still shown, receipts, cards and a typed bank figure move as real; sample plan values never move [Y16]. Two devices' moves merge like any records, the newer `at` winning per record [Y12]; a moved record's `at` is the copy's last save, and a device that finds a moved copy in the cloud takes it [AB1]. What waits for a pay, bill or payment day, or for the loan question, is kept in `pendingMove` and written when it is answered [AA5, AB rows, AC2]. Moved pay is `per: 'month'`, and a one-month amount replaces that month's total, spread over that month's pays or periods [AB2, AC1]; a moved bill answered with another rhythm than once a month is `per: 'month'` too, its one-month amount spread over that month's payments [AD4]. The cases are `rules/move.json` (mv-01 to mv-108).
