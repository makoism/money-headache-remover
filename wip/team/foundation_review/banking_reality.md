**Banking reality check of the Burrow model (read-only; branch wip-38.31)**

I read NORTH_STAR, DECISIONS, model.js, the screens that matter here, and two designs on side branches: LEDGER_DESIGN.md (side/ledger-design) and PAYDAY_DESIGN.md (side/payday-design). Copies of both are in /tmp/banking/. I wrote nothing in the repo.

**What I found overall:** the 38.31 engine is one pool of money that runs month by month. It starts from a balance it treats as "the bank on the 1st, before pay" (model.js:318, 381). Pay, bills and goal money come with no dates (model.js:376-377). Receipts are grouped by calendar month (model.js:574). Almost every gap below comes from that. The ledger design already fixes about half of them (accounts, places, refunds, pay-later, other currencies, pay-to-pay months, no window). The payday design stores pay as days of the month only, so it cannot hold pay every two weeks or every week.

**The 5 most important foundation changes, in order**

1. **Dated ledger with accounts and an "as of" balance.** Today's typed balance becomes a dated opening balance per account. Every flow is a dated event, and what is safe to spend is worked out from today forward. This fixes pay counted twice, Check your bank vs the plan start (MAKO_RUN 1.1), e-wallets, cash and transfers. Burrows only hold money that was really moved, or set aside in an account named for it. This is ledger B1, plus a link from each burrow to an account, which the design does not have yet.
2. **A pay schedule, not a list of days.** Save how often (monthly, twice a month, every 2 weeks, weekly, irregular), a start date, the days, the amount per pay and a working-day rule. A month view becomes "pay to next pay". Save this shape before `payDays` ships, so it never needs a migration.
3. **Cards as accounts with a statement close day and a due day.** Each card spend lands on a statement and a due date. Refunds are money in. Instalments are planned parts that flow into statements. Interest applies only to what is carried over. The minimum is a percent or a fixed amount.
4. **Bills as schedules.** A due day, a frequency (every N months or a list of months, e.g. 10 parts a year) and an estimated or fixed amount. Non-monthly bills get a set-aside each pay, and direct debits follow the working-day rule. Repeats gain every 2 weeks and the last day of the month.
5. **Time and units.** Drop the fixed START/END and use a rolling window. Guard against a wrong clock before writing anything. Save the currency as an ISO code and amounts as whole minor units, so the yen vs cents rule comes from a table, not from the '¥' symbol.

Items 2 to 4 are not in either design. Item 5's currency part is already in the ledger design.
