## The three values (worked out once, in lookahead.js, the same meaning in every life)
All in the home currency, whole minor units, from the engine's own walk. `lastDay` is the last day everyday money runs to: the day BEFORE the next counted pay lands (one day before `safeToSpend.until`, which is the pay day itself) or, with no fixed pay, the last day of this calendar month (BQ3); null when no pay is known and the plan does not run by the day.
1. `res.canSpare = { amount, lastDay, through }`: what the home-currency spend-from accounts can spare after everything due, goals kept in them, plan lines, the minimum, and the everyday money of the days AFTER `lastDay` that the walk already holds. It is NOT reduced by everyday money up to `lastDay`. Signed: below zero when short. `through` is the horizon day the walk looks to (res.horizon), so a screen can say how far ahead it looks. With a pay period this is today's `raw` (the sum of the accounts' headroom), unchanged. With no fixed pay it is the same walk with the Free to spend by-day items dated on or before `lastDay` left out (only the account that holds the everyday line is touched).
2. `res.everyday = { full, counts, spent, left, lastDay, kind: 'pay' | 'month' }`: `full` is the everyday amount as set (period.free.amount's meaning), `counts` the part of it that counts for this stretch (less than full when the person started partway), `left` what is left, signed (below zero when over), and `spent` is `counts` less `left`, exactly as periods.js works it (so fees, money placed into it and adjustments are inside it and counts - spent = left always). `null` when no everyday amount is set. An everyday amount of 0 is an amount: `{ full: 0, counts: 0, spent, left }`, never null.
3. `res.noJob = { amount }`: `canSpare.amount` less `everyday.left` where that is above zero, never below zero. So when nothing is short and everyday is not over: canSpare = everyday.left + noJob, to the yen. With no everyday amount: noJob = max(0, canSpare).
