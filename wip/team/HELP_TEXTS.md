# The Burrow: help texts (the words behind every "?")

Lane H2 of LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md. Written by SIDE K, 2026-11-19. Words only: nothing here is wired into the app yet.

**How to use this file.** One entry per idea a person could get stuck on. Each entry has a title in the app's own word, "Why it matters" (the mindset), "What to do" (the function, never where a button sits, because screens will change) and, where it helps, a tiny example with made-up pesos. Each is short enough for Mako to read aloud in one breath of a video. The text under "Why it matters" and "What to do" is exactly what the "?" shows, in that order.

**Rules kept.** The app's own words (burrow, goal, Free to spend, Giving, "not placed yet", Total, Bills left, Saved). No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no jargon, no shame. Made-up numbers only.

**Notes for the build.**

The Wallet and Track default is one of Mako's "notes to look at" (LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md), so entry 23 is written to stay true whichever is the default.
- Entry 9 uses "Rainy Day" as the person-facing name of the emergency fund, as the brief asks. The app today says "Emergency fund". Pick one name when this is wired in and make the entry match.
- The place where new pay waits on pay day has no word yet (DECISIONS.md, 2026-11-19), so entry 15 does not name it.

## 1. What is in your bank right now

**Why it matters.** Every other number here starts from the one true thing: how much is really in your bank today. If we guess, everything after it is a guess too. You are not being tested. It is just the starting line.

**What to do.** Open your bank app and type the number it shows. Do it again whenever you want to check that the Burrow still matches real life.

**Example.** Your bank app says ₱18,400. Type ₱18,400. That is all.

---

## 2. Now and planned

**Why it matters.** Some numbers are money you really have. Others are money you have decided to use. People mix the two up all the time, and that is where the "wait, where did it go?" feeling comes from. Seeing which is which is half the calm.

**What to do.** Read "now" as what is in your hands today, and "planned" as what you have told the money to do. If a number says planned, it is not in your account yet.

**Example.** "Now" says ₱18,400 in the bank. "Planned" says ₱6,000 of your pay is set for rent. The rent money is still in the bank, but it already has a job.

---

## 3. Total

**Why it matters.** Total is everything you have, in one number, wherever it sits. It is the one you check when you just want to know if you are okay. It does not say what you can spend. That is a different number, and it has its own name.

**What to do.** Look at Total to see the whole picture, then look at Free to spend to see what is yours to use. If Total looks wrong, check your bank figure first.

**Example.** Total ₱36,000 might be ₱18,000 in the bank, ₱15,000 in burrows and ₱3,000 in your wallet. Same money, three places.

---

## 4. Free to spend

**Why it matters.** This is the money that is really yours to use, after your bills and your savings are taken care of. People who feel calm about money are not spending less. They know exactly which pesos are free, so they can enjoy them without guilt. When Free to spend is gone, it is gone until next time, and that is fine.

**What to do.** Spend from it without worrying about the rest. If it runs low, slow down for the rest of the month, or move a little from somewhere you are comfortable taking it.

**Example.** Free to spend ₱4,000 for the month. A ₱350 dinner with friends is simply ₱350 of your ₱4,000.

---

## 5. Bills left

**Why it matters.** Bills are the things that will happen whether you feel like it or not. Seeing what is still to pay this month means you never get surprised by them, and you never spend money that already belongs to the landlord.

**What to do.** Keep that money where it is until the bill is paid. When a bill is paid, log it and the number goes down.

**Example.** Bills left ₱9,500: electricity ₱2,000, internet ₱1,500 and rent ₱6,000 are still coming. Paying the internet takes it to ₱8,000.

---

## 6. Saved

**Why it matters.** Saved is the money you have set aside for later: your goals, your rainy day, the things that pile up over months. This number only means something if you do not dip into it for ordinary days. It is a quiet number, and it is the one that grows your calm.

**What to do.** Leave Saved alone, and let it grow a little every pay day. When you do need it, take it on purpose and say what it is for.

**Example.** Saved ₱45,000 across your burrows. This month you add ₱3,000, so next month it reads ₱48,000.

---

## 7. A burrow

**Why it matters.** A burrow is a place where money piles up over months, for something that matters to you. A trip, a home, a rainy day, school, giving, investing. Money with a name is much harder to spend by accident. That is the whole trick.

**What to do.** Make a burrow for anything you want to build up to. Give it a name you care about, and add a little every pay day.

**Example.** A burrow called "Trip to Siargao". Every pay day, ₱1,500 goes in. It fills slowly, and you can see it filling.

---

## 8. A goal

**Why it matters.** A goal is the amount a burrow is aiming for. It turns "I should save more" into a number you can reach. Without one, saving feels endless. With one, you can see the finish line, and finishing is a good feeling.

**What to do.** Give a burrow a goal amount, and a date if you have one. The Burrow works out how much to add each month.

**Example.** Trip goal ₱30,000 in 10 months. That is ₱3,000 a month. If the date is further away, the monthly amount gets smaller.

---

## 9. Rainy Day (the emergency fund)

**Why it matters.** Life sends surprises: a broken phone, a sick relative, a quiet month at work. People who have a rainy day fund do not panic when one arrives, because it is already paid for. It is the first burrow worth building, before the fun ones. It is the one that turns a crisis into an inconvenience.

**What to do.** Start small, even ₱500 a month. Aim first for about three months of your bills, and only use it for real surprises.

**Example.** Your bills are ₱20,000 a month. A first goal of ₱60,000 means three calm months if anything goes wrong.

---

## 10. Education

**Why it matters.** Education is money you put into learning: a course, a book, a skill. It is a burrow, so it piles up and you spend from it on purpose. Money spent on getting better at something tends to come back to you. That is why it has its own place.

**What to do.** Put in a small amount each month, and take from it when you sign up for something you want to learn.

**Example.** ₱500 a month adds up to ₱6,000 a year, enough for a good online course.

---

## 11. Giving

**Why it matters.** Giving is the money you keep for gifts and for people and causes you care about. When it has its own burrow, giving feels like a plan instead of a surprise, and you never have to say no from guilt or yes from panic. It piles up, and you spend from the pile.

**What to do.** Put a little in every month, and when a birthday or a cause comes, give from the pile. If the pile is empty, it is okay to give less this time.

**Example.** ₱800 a month goes into Giving. In December, a family party and two gifts take ₱3,000 from a pile that has grown to ₱6,400.

---

## 12. Investing

**Why it matters.** Investing is money you put away to grow, and you do not spend it. It is for the far future, not for next month. That is why it is kept apart from everything else, so it is not touched on a hard day.

**What to do.** Add what you can, on a regular day, and leave it. It only grows here. If you are not ready to invest yet, an empty one is fine.

**Example.** ₱1,000 on every pay day. After a year, ₱12,000 is waiting for the future you.

---

## 13. What you owe

**Why it matters.** Owing money is normal. What hurts is not seeing it. Putting what you owe in the picture does not make you a worse person, it just makes the picture true. Once it is visible, you can make a plan for it, and plans are calming.

**What to do.** Add each card or loan with what you owe now. Pay it down when you can and watch the number get smaller.

**Example.** A card that owes ₱8,200. You pay ₱2,000. It now owes ₱6,200, and the ₱2,000 is not lost, it is gone from what you owe.

---

## 14. Card spending is already spent

**Why it matters.** When you pay with a card, the money does not leave the bank that day. It leaves when you pay the card. So the bank can look fuller than it really is. The Burrow keeps the card spending apart so you do not spend the same peso twice.

**What to do.** Type what is on each card now, and when it is paid. The Burrow then shows what your bank will hold after the cards are paid. That is the number that is really yours.

**Example.** Bank ₱25,000. Cards ₱9,000. After the cards are paid you have ₱16,000, so that is what you really have.

---

## 15. Pay day, and giving every coin a place

**Why it matters.** Money with no job drifts away, and most people never see where it went. On pay day, you decide what each peso is for before you can spend it. That one habit is most of what money handled feels like. It is not about having more. It is about knowing.

**What to do.** When pay lands, enter it and let the Burrow help you share it across bills, burrows and Free to spend. Anything not placed yet is still yours, so give it a place when you are ready.

**Example.** Pay ₱30,000. Bills ₱14,000, burrows ₱6,000, Free to spend ₱10,000. Nothing is floating.

---

## 16. Logging a receipt

**Why it matters.** Writing down what you spend is how you see where money goes. It is not to catch yourself out. It is just looking, and people who look usually end up spending in ways they like better.

**What to do.** Add what you spent, with the amount and a rough category. Rough is fine. You can also upload a statement and let the Burrow read it for you.

**Example.** ₱280 on groceries, logged in ten seconds. Free to spend goes down by ₱280, and that is all that happened.

---

## 17. Why once a week is enough

**Why it matters.** You do not need to log every coin the moment you spend it. That would wear anyone out, and then people quit. A short check once a week is enough to stay true, and enough to build a habit that lasts for years.

**What to do.** Pick a day that is easy, such as a Sunday evening. Log what you remember, check your bank figure, and be done. Under a minute is fine.

**Example.** Sunday, 9 pm. Three receipts, one bank check, a cup of tea. Done until next week.

---

## 18. The month-end question

**Why it matters.** At the end of the month, some money may be left that you planned to use and did not. If it just drops out of sight, you lose track of it, and knowing where your money is matters most. So the Burrow asks you to choose where it goes.

**What to do.** Pick a place for what is left: a goal, your rainy day, or keep it as Free to spend. If you are not sure, choose Decide later. It will wait for you.

**Example.** You planned ₱4,000 of Free to spend and spent ₱3,100. The ₱900 left can go to your trip burrow, and next month it is still ₱900 closer.

---

## 19. Below zero

**Why it matters.** Sometimes the bank goes under what you planned, or even below zero. It happens to almost everyone, and it is not a verdict on you. It is just information: something this month cost more than the plan, and the Burrow shows you what and when.

**What to do.** Read the line the Burrow shows, see which month and how much, and decide whether to spend less, move money, or wait for the next pay. There is no rush and no wrong feeling about it.

**Example.** The bank would be ₱1,200 below zero on the 8th, before pay on the 10th. You can hold back ₱1,200 of spending, or move it from Free to spend.

---

## 20. The cloud copy and the recovery code

**Why it matters.** Your numbers live on this phone. If the phone is lost or changed, they go with it, unless you keep a copy. The cloud copy is that copy, locked so only you can open it. The recovery code is the only key, so it is worth a minute now to be safe later.

**What to do.** Turn on the cloud copy, then write the recovery code down or save it somewhere you trust, away from the phone. On a new phone, type the code and your numbers come back.

**Example.** Your phone falls in the sea. A new phone, the recovery code, and two minutes later everything is back as it was.

---

## 21. A backup file

**Why it matters.** A backup file is your own copy that you hold yourself. It is a second safety net, and it costs nothing. Money habits stay easy when you know you cannot lose them.

**What to do.** Save a backup file now and then, especially before big changes. Keep it somewhere you can find it, such as your email or a drive.

---

## 22. Wallet and Track

**Why it matters.** People handle their spending money in two ways, and both are fine. Some like to set a sum and spend from it without counting each coin. Others like to see every coin. Pick the one you will actually keep up.

**What to do.** Choose Wallet if you want to spend your Free to spend freely and only check in now and then. Choose Track if you want to log each purchase against it. You can change your mind later.

**Example.** Wallet: ₱4,000 set aside for the month, spent however you like. Track: each coffee and ride is logged and the number goes down as you go.

---

## 23. Borrowed, lent and paid back

**Why it matters.** Money moves between people, and it should not be a mystery. If you borrow, you owe it. If you lend, someone owes you. Writing it down is kind to both of you, and it keeps the friendship easy.

**What to do.** Use Borrowed when money comes in that you will give back, Lent when you pay for someone, and Paid back when they return it or you return it. The Burrow then keeps your picture true.

**Example.** You pay ₱1,500 for a friend's share of dinner. Mark it Lent. When they send it back, mark it Paid back, and it is even.

---

## 24. Sending money to family

**Why it matters.** Many people send money home every month, and some months a surprise ask comes too. This is a real part of life for a lot of us, not a leak to be ashamed of. Giving it a line of its own means it is planned, so it never has to come out of your bills by surprise.

**What to do.** Add Family support as its own line, with the amount you usually send. When a surprise ask comes, log it there and see what it did to the month.

**Example.** ₱5,000 a month home, planned. A surprise ₱2,000 for school fees comes out of Free to spend, and you can see it plainly.

---

## 25. A missed week

**Why it matters.** Everyone skips a week, or a month. Busy, tired, a rough patch. It does not undo anything, and nothing is lost. You do not owe the app an apology and there is nothing to catch up on.

**What to do.** Choose Start fresh from today. Type what is in your bank now, and carry on from here. The gap is just a gap.

**Example.** Three weeks without logging. You type today's bank figure, ₱12,300, and the Burrow is true again in under a minute.

---

## 26. Sample numbers

**Why it matters.** Until you type your own numbers, what you see is made-up, there so you can see how the Burrow works. It is only a picture to look at, not your money, and nothing here is a judgment.

**What to do.** Replace the sample with your own pay, bills and bank figure. After that, everything on screen is yours.

---

## 27. The status line (On plan, Tight, Short)

**Why it matters.** This is the Burrow's honest one-line answer to "am I okay this month?" On plan means yes. Tight means yes, with little room. Short means the plan asks for more than you have, and a small change now is easier than a surprise later. None of them is a grade.

**What to do.** If it says Tight or Short, open the month and look at what could give a little, such as Free to spend. The Burrow offers one-tap ways to fix it.

**Example.** Short by ₱1,000. Trim Free to spend by ₱1,000, and the month is On plan again.

---

## 28. Not placed yet

**Why it matters.** This is pay that has not been given a job. It is not lost, it is not wrong, it is just undecided. Undecided money is the easiest to spend without noticing, so it is worth giving it a place.

**What to do.** Add it to a burrow, to Free to spend, or to a bill. Or leave it for now, because it is still in the bank and still yours.

**Example.** ₱2,500 not placed yet. Put ₱1,500 in your trip burrow and ₱1,000 in Free to spend, and it is all placed.

## Reread: the beginner pass

Read as someone who never learned to save, these were changed so no one feels small or lost:

- Every entry leads with how it helps, never with what the person did wrong. Words like "should", "must" and "you need to" were taken out.
- A person with ₱0 in a burrow, or nothing yet in Investing, is told that is fine (entries 12, 11).
- Below zero, Short, Tight, a missed week and unplaced pay are all named as information, never as failure (entries 19, 25, 27, 28).
- Family support and borrowing are called normal life, not a leak (entries 23, 24).
- Every term that could stop someone (burrow, goal, Free to spend, planned, not placed yet) has its own entry in plain words, with one made-up example in pesos.
